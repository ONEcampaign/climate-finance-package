# climate-finance 2.0.0

Version 2.0 moves the package from local matching to versioned releases. Each release holds climate commitments from the OECD's CRDF, the CRS disbursements traced to them, and the evidence for each link. `cf.load()` opens a release, and `Release.spending()` answers a query with a low and a high bound. [Migrating from 1.x](../migrating-from-1x.md) maps each 1.x call to its replacement.

Totals from 2.0 differ from 1.2.3 and from the OECD's own climate totals. The figures on this page come from a build of CRS version v20261006 and every CRDF commitment year the OECD publishes, with the settings `build.toml` ships. Unless a sentence says otherwise, a figure is in USD under ONE's method, in the recipient view.

## The build

`release.json` records every build switch and year range under `settings`. This release ran with `key_repair`, `recoveries`, `review`, `jev_links`, and `jev_flags` on, with bank ledgers configured for the ledger check, and with `gap_fill`, `jev_labels`, and `embed` off. With the fill stage off, the release holds no bank-ledger or IATI fill rows and withholds nothing.

The recipient-perspective CRDF starts in 2000, and the CRS records the build reads start with it. Before 2010 the CRDF carries no adaptation marker and no climate components, so adaptation for those years is null and their climate value is mitigation alone. The provider-perspective CRDF starts in 2013, so the provider view attributes core contributions from 2013 on.

## Why totals differ from 1.x

1.x matched each CRDF row to CRS rows on a concatenated key of provider, agency, project id, title, recipient, finance type, modality, purpose, and year, trying five keys in turn and dropping fields from the later ones. Each key that matched ran again without the year, so a later disbursement counted where it carried the same ids as the matched record. 2.0 links a disbursement to its commitment in any year, including under ids the donor changed, allocates it, counts the Rio-marked disbursement that reaches no commitment as untraced, and reports what it could not allocate in the `unallocated` table.

Other changes move smaller amounts:

- Agency shares for ONE's attribution rest on the agency's climate commitments over its CRS commitments, summed over the share year and the year before. 1.x divided matched climate disbursements by all CRS disbursements, and read zero wherever matching failed.
- A CRDF row with a negative face value, a published downward revision, stays in `commitments` as published and has nothing disbursed.
- The CRS writes "Developing countries, unspecified" as 9998 and the CRDF as 998. Every release table uses 998.
- Private-sector and philanthropic flows (Bi_Multi 6) stay out of the views.
- CRDF rows carry no Bi_Multi code. The build sets it to the provider's most common Bi_Multi among its non-core CRS records, and to 2 for imputed multilateral contributions.

## Which disbursements count

A disbursement counts against a commitment the release holds, and the release's commitments start at the first CRDF year. A disbursement in an early year often pays a commitment from before that year, so the years nearest the start count less of what was paid than later years do. Disbursements end at `crs_last_complete`, the last CRS year every provider has reported.

Rio-marked CRS disbursement that reaches no commitment also counts, as untraced disbursement weighted by the record's own markers: a linked record's disbursement beyond what its commitments take, and the whole of a record linked to none. It counts in `value` and `high` and not in `low`, and the result's `untraced` column holds it. `untraced=False` gives the traced figures alone. With `gap_fill` on, the GEF's and the GCF's unlinked records stay out, since their IATI fills may count the same money. Disbursements linked to CRS commitments with no CRDF row in the release count nowhere, and `unallocated` holds them.

## Provider and recipient views

The two views reconcile in four lines. For disbursements in 2015 to 2024, the recipient-view total of USD 386.05bn, less USD 169.36bn of multilateral agencies' own outflows, less USD 28.90bn of EU Institutions' own rows, plus USD 99.15bn of attributed core contributions, gives the provider-view total of USD 286.94bn.

The EIB counts as a channel for its members' money under both EU settings, so an all-provider total leaves its own lending out of the provider view, USD 10.2bn of disbursements and USD 27.0bn of commitments in 2015 to 2024. Core contributions to channels with no climate share, among them the Global Fund, Gavi, UNDP, and UNEP, attribute nothing.

## Coefficients

The significant coefficient moves `value` more than any choice of link. Under OECD's weights, which count significant markers at 100%, disbursements in 2015 to 2024 total USD 503.8bn, with a `low` of USD 380.6bn, against ONE's USD 386.0bn. Each 0.1 of the significant coefficient moves `value` by about USD 19.6bn.

`PROVIDER` takes the coefficients each provider applies when it reports to the UNFCCC, from the OECD's survey DCD/DAC/STAT(2024)28/REV1, with Ireland before 2021 and Luxembourg read from their own reports. It gives USD 401.6bn for the same disbursements. `provider_coefficients` names the source of each coefficient.

## Grant equivalents

`flow="grant_equivalent"` values each disbursement at the grant equivalent the CRS gives its record. The CRS gives none for many grants of the development banks and funds. The build values a grant, CRS finance type 110 (standard grant) or 210 (interest subsidy), that has no grant equivalent at its face value, and keeps the CRS figure wherever one exists. Every table built from the records, `disbursements`, `untraced`, and `total_flows` among them, carries the same value, so `share_of_total()` divides by a total that holds the same grants.

A loan, or any other record that is not a grant, with no grant equivalent counts 0 in `value`, `low`, and `high`. The result's `no_grant_equivalent` column holds its climate value at face, beside `untraced`, so the money left out shows beside every total. It is 0 under every other flow.

## Oxfam's bounds

`cf.methods.OXFAM_LOW` and `cf.methods.OXFAM_HIGH` follow the bounds of Oxfam's Climate-Specific Net Assistance, and `cf.methods.OXFAM` runs the two together. The low bound counts significant markers at 30% and principal markers at 85%. The high bound takes each provider's own coefficients capped at 50% and 100%, through the new setting `Method(coefficients="capped")`. Oxfam values commitments at an estimated grant element and attributes multilateral outflows with the OECD's shares. The package values disbursements at their own grant equivalent and attributes through each agency's own climate spending. [About the methods](../explanation/methods.md#oxfams-method) lists the rules the package follows in part.

## What `low` counts

`low` counts the money on links the build treats as certain by rule. Among them are the parts of rebuilt aggregate rows, which match their CRS records by amount within 0.1% to 1% and carry USD 4.3bn of `low`. The commitments the recovery routes (`recoveries`) reach, those parts among them, carry USD 6.6bn of `low`.

In the provider view, `low` counts attributed core contributions in full, since they are the donor's own reported payments. The agency's climate share is a method parameter, and method parameters, coefficients and agency shares alike, apply to `low`, `value`, and `high` together, so `low` bounds link certainty alone. Attributed contributions hold USD 99.2bn of the provider view's USD 246.9bn `low`.

## Net flows

`flow="net_disbursements"` subtracts from gross disbursements the principal repaid on the loans they disbursed, and `flow="net_transfers"` subtracts the interest too. The build keeps the CRS's `usd_received` and `usd_interest` up to `crs_last_complete` in the `reflows` table, links each repayment to its loan through the trace's exact key tiers, and splits it over the parts of the loan's disbursement. A repayment subtracts at the share its loan was counted under, so a loan counted at 40% climate repays at 40%, and a development bank's loan repays at its climate share. Where gross counts a loan's disbursements as untraced, their repayments subtract at the same record markers.

Repayments tied to no counted disbursement subtract nothing, and `release.quality().repayments` reports each by basis with the `measured_share` that reaches a total. Spain's loans, disbursed before the CRS years the build reads, fall outside, and so does the EBRD, which files no project number and a new crs id for each repayment.

`low` subtracts every attributed repayment and `high` only those whose link to the loan is certain, so for a net flow `high` sits above `value`. The development banks report no interest after 2018, so from 2019 their net transfers equal their net disbursements. Under `PROVIDER`, a repayment weighs at the coefficients of the year it is repaid. A net flow refuses `measure="share_of_total"` and `relevance="jev"`, and `flow="grant_equivalent"` is gross. The `reflow_conservation` gate checks that the repayments the CRS reports reach the release by provider and year, for principal and for interest. [About the methods](../explanation/methods.md#net-flows) and [Low and high bounds](../explanation/low-and-high-bounds.md#net-flows) describe the method and the interval.

## ONE's 2024 headline figures

ONE published two headline figures in African Arguments in January 2024: "between 2013 and 2021, $228 billion in climate finance commitments had not been disbursed", and for "an additional $69 billion" ONE "couldn't even find disbursements data". Neither figure had a recorded method, so the difference from 2.0 cannot be split into causes line by line.

The 2.0 trace restates both for 2013 to 2021.

| Status                                             | Climate commitments, USD bn |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| `disbursed` (at least 90% of face value allocated) | 153.6                       |
| `partly_disbursed`                                 | 219.0                       |
| `no_disbursement_record`                           | 39.4                        |
| `not_traceable`                                    | 84.0                        |
| `pending_review`                                   | 7.5                         |

The 90% threshold that splits `disbursed` from `partly_disbursed` is a definition. Together the two statuses hold USD 372.6bn, which is the figure to quote where the split is not the point.

The USD 228bn figure becomes the undisbursed climate value of commitments with a disbursement record: the climate value of the `disbursed` and `partly_disbursed` commitments less the climate value the trace allocated to them, USD 119.0bn. It is measured at the end of `crs_last_complete`, 2024, and much of it is money still to be paid. The 2013 to 2018 commitments hold USD 53.0bn of it and the 2019 to 2021 commitments USD 66.1bn. The 2013 and 2014 commitments have reached 75.9% of their face value, and if later commitments follow them, about a quarter of the remainder is loan pipeline. The World Bank holds USD 34.0bn of the remainder across 2,208 loans. On the 1,493 of them its loan ledger matches at the end of 2024, which hold USD 28.2bn, 68% is undisbursed on loans still disbursing and 25% was cancelled, taking each loan's ledger split of its unpaid face value. Counting certain links alone, the remainder is USD 170.6bn. The remainder plus the `no_disbursement_record` and `not_traceable` commitments, USD 242.4bn, rises by 21% when only certain links count, and the remainder alone by 43%.

The USD 69bn figure becomes the climate value of commitments with no disbursement found, the `no_disbursement_record` and `not_traceable` rows above, which stay separate. A commitment is `not_traceable` when it reaches no CRS commitment, when its agency files neither a crs id nor a project number that persists from year to year, or when too little of its agency's disbursement value carries a usable key. The EBRD, IFAD, and CAF, among others, fail that last test, so their commitments with no disbursement record count as `not_traceable`.

`not_traceable` marks a gap in donor reporting and says nothing about payment, so do not add it to `no_disbursement_record` as unpaid. USD 32.0bn of it is 20 annual aggregate rows from IFC, IDB Invest, and the NDB, which the CRS holds only as aggregates, and the AfDB's ledger shows money paid on AfDB projects the trace cannot follow.

## How sure the links are

A blind sample measures each provider's link precision. Agents label every draw, people decide at least one draw in ten, and the gate requires the lower bound of each provider's interval to reach 0.90. `cf-build publish` publishes a release only when its `release.json` records every gate and each row passed, or when `cf-build release --waive-precision REASON` waived the `precision` and `human_audit` gates and recorded the reason in `release.json`. `--waive-precision` takes the reason as its value, so a script that passes the bare flag must add one.

`release.quality().precision` reports each provider's estimate, its interval, and whether it passed. `release.json` records each stratum's estimate and interval beside the provider's under `gates.precision`, so estimated links carry a figure of their own.

## Jev labels

`Method(relevance="jev")` needs a release built with `jev_labels` on. This release holds no Jev labels, so a query with `relevance="jev"` raises `MissingLabelsError`.

## Jev flags

`release.jev_flags()` needs a release built with `jev_flags` on, and raises `MissingLabelsError` otherwise. This release holds Jev flags for 2015 to 2024. Jev answers four questions about each climate-marked CRS record, and the table flags records with no climate aim (`no_aim`, precision 1.00 and recall 0.93 on 200 hand-labelled texts) and records a reader would not expect to count (`surprising`, precision 0.83 and recall 0.60 on 240), rates measured on the texts the questions were revised against. The answer on counterproductive activities ranks records only, and the mitigation answer has no external check and comes only with `experimental=True`. The flags change no total. [About the methods](../explanation/methods.md#jev-flags) has the detail.

## Licences

The figures in a release are an adaptation of OECD data and carry no endorsement by the OECD.

UNFCCC documents are public domain where no change to the content is introduced and the source is acknowledged. The release ships BTR reconciliation aggregates and leaves out the parsed BTR rows and their links. [How to reproduce the BTR tables](../how-to/reproduce-btr-tables.md) builds them locally from the workbooks.

The Green Climate Fund's website terms allow use for private study, research, and teaching, and bar commercial derivative works without written consent. Its projects API states no terms of its own. Disbursement rows built from the API and the readiness list are withheld from a release. `commitments.withheld_usd` states what they disbursed to each commitment, which the commitment's `disbursed_usd` and the `quality` table include and `spending()` figures leave out. Withheld rows come from the fill stage, so this release withholds nothing. The Green Climate Fund's IATI files are CC0 on the IATI Registry, and their rows ship.

`sources.json` in each pack lists every source with its licence and a note on rows left out. [How linking works](../explanation/how-linking-works.md) and [Release tables](../reference/release-tables.md) have the detail.

## Currency and prices

A release ships its conversion factors in the `conversion_factors` table: the OECD DAC exchange rates and inverse deflators that pydeflate gave when the build fetched its sources, for USD, EUR, GBP, and CAD at every base year the DAC exchange rates cover, and for EUR, GBP, and CAD at current prices. A query converts with them and makes no network call, so a saved `Spec` run on the same release gives the same converted figure. `result.meta.conversion` holds the table's SHA-256 and a SHA-256 of the rows a result read. pydeflate is a dependency of the `build` extra only.

## New in 2.0

- `cf.load()`, `Release.spending()`, `quality()`, `reconcile()`, and `disbursement_profile()`. [Python API](../reference/python-api.md) describes them.
- `cf.methods.ONE`, `OECD`, `PROVIDER`, `RANGE`, `OXFAM_LOW`, `OXFAM_HIGH`, and `OXFAM`, and `Method` for your own coefficients, with `relevance="jev"` to read Jev's adaptation levels.
- `result.rows()`, `result.explain()`, `result.share_of_total()`, and `Spec` for tracing and rerunning a figure. `cf.compare()` sets results side by side.
- `release.reconcile()`, which sets a party's BTR total against OECD figures.
- `release.jev_flags()`, which returns Jev's flags on climate-marked CRS records for review.
- `flow="net_disbursements"` and `flow="net_transfers"`, which subtract the principal and interest repaid on climate loans, and `release.quality().repayments`. [How to compare gross and net climate finance](../how-to/compare-gross-and-net-climate-finance.md) shows both.
- `cf-build`, which builds, reviews, gates, packs, and publishes releases. `cf-build publish` uploads a pack to the GitHub release tagged `data` and replaces `releases.json` there. [cf-build](../reference/cf-build.md) lists its commands.
- Spending tables with `flow`, `view`, `currency`, `prices`, and `measure` columns that repeat the query on every row, and `no_adaptation_marker`, the part of `value` with no adaptation split because the row rests on a year before 2010. A query under several methods, as `cf.methods.RANGE`, adds `range_low` and `range_high`, the lowest `low` and the highest `high` of the methods.
- `ClimateFinanceWarning`, the base of every warning, so one filter turns them all into errors. `CoverageWarning` names years and providers a query finds no rows for, `DoubleCountWarning` flags provider-view results that count EU money twice when added, and `ClimateFinanceDeprecationWarning` marks the 1.x names. [Python API](../reference/python-api.md) lists them.
- A `code` on every error, such as `unknown_provider` for `UnknownProviderError`, shown in brackets at the end of the message. Match the class or the code, since the wording can change.
- `cf.providers.list()` adds `type` and `mdb` (true for the 17 multilateral development banks), `cf.recipients.list()` adds `type`, and `providers="World Bank"` names IBRD and IDA together.

## Removed or changed

`ClimateData` runs with a `ClimateFinanceDeprecationWarning` until 3.0 for every 1.x choice whose meaning 2.0 keeps, and its `value` column holds climate value only. A choice whose meaning 2.0 changed raises `RemovedError` naming the 2.0 call. These are `flows="net_disbursements"`, which 2.0 nets by loan, `source="OECD_CRS"`, the 1.x default (so `load_spending_data()` without `source=` raises too), and the other sources that [Migrating from 1.x](../migrating-from-1x.md#choices-that-changed-meaning) lists. `set_climate_finance_data_path` imports and runs with the same warning and sets the directory `ClimateData` downloads releases into. `get_unfccc_data` imports with the warning and raises `RemovedError` naming `release.reconcile()` when called. The other removed calls and modules raise `RemovedError` naming their replacement. The package requires Python 3.12 or later.
